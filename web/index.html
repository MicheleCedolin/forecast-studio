1Data

How should an Excel or CSV file look?

Either of these, with a header row; the page works out which one it is and converts the table on this device.

  • One row per date: a date column, then one column per series (for example date, shop A, shop B).
  • One row per observation: three columns for the series name, the date and the value (for example series, date, value).
  • No dates: only columns of numbers, one per series, oldest value first; choose the frequency yourself.

Dates may be written as 2024-01-31, 31.01.2024, 2024-01, Jan 2024, 2024 Q1 or a plain year, or be Excel date cells. Each series needs one value per period (day, week, month …); dates that are absent become missing values. In an Excel file the first sheet that holds a table is read. Old .xls files are not read: save them as .xlsx or CSV.

More data: download any dataset of the Monash Time Series Forecasting Archive (files on Zenodo), unzip it and choose the .tsf file above. Your own file is read on this device and is not uploaded. Large files need a lot of memory; on a phone stay with files of a few megabytes. The examples are from this archive (CC BY 4.0).

Demand pattern of the series

Classes of Syntetos, Boylan and Croston (2005): a series is intermittent or lumpy when demand occurs rarely (average interval between non-zero values of 1.32 or more), and erratic or lumpy when the non-zero values vary a lot (squared coefficient of variation of 0.49 or more).

What a run does

Each series is split in two: the last horizon observations are set aside as the test part, everything before them is the training part. The method sees only the training part and forecasts the horizon; the forecast is then compared with the test part, one error value per series for each measure.

Series too short for the split are left out and listed in the result. When a method fails on a series, that series receives the seasonal naive forecast instead; these fallbacks are counted and reported, never hidden.

Forecast the future skips the test: the method sees the whole series and forecasts the steps after its last observation, so there is nothing to compare the forecast with and no error is computed. With ten series or more the page also gives 80% and 95% prediction intervals. They are split conformal intervals: the same method is fitted once more without the last horizon observations, its errors on those observations (scaled per series, pooled over the series, one set per step ahead) say how far off such a forecast usually is, and that spread is placed around the new forecast. They assume the future errs like the recent past.

Nothing on this tab runs a computation. The texts are the ones the desktop application shows for the same methods and measures.

Error measures

Every measure is computed per series; the summary gives the mean and the median over the series. A value that is undefined for a series (for example a division by zero) is left out and counted, not replaced.
